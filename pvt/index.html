Sampling conditions (optional — for the representativity checks)

2Rank the correlations

Each correlation in the library is swapped in, tuned to the data and scored by its average absolute relative error (AARE). Untuned is the published correlation; tuned is after regression. The chart beside each table plots every candidate against the laboratory points. Choose the correlation to carry into tuning with the buttons in the first column — the best fit is preselected only when you ask for it.

3Tune and apply

1PVT samples

One entry per PVT report from this reservoir — bottomhole samples, separator recombinations, repeat studies. Each keeps its own oil gravity, gas gravity, Rsb and temperature; all use the correlations selected in the case inputs. Enter separator-adjusted (flash-corrected) Rs and Bo, not raw differential-liberation values. The sampling pressures, producing GOR and bottomhole sampling depth are optional but drive the representativity and depth-trend checks.

2Screen the samples

Every sample is checked on its own (representativity, trends, Bo–Rs consistency, points out of line) and against the others: a sample biased away from the correlation differently from its neighbours, or off the depth trend of the other bottomhole samples, is a bad sample or a different fluid. Samples that fail are left out of the fit; override with the Use box. Select a sample to read its checks.

3Fit the correlations to the accepted samples

One multiplier per property, shared by every accepted sample and fitted to all their points together — a field-wide correction rather than one sample's. Ranking swaps in every correlation and scores it on the pooled data.

4Apply to the case

The multipliers act on the fluid in the case inputs. To build the tables for one of the samples, make it the case: its oil gravity, gas gravity, Rsb and temperature go to the case inputs, and its laboratory data to the one-report table.

What this tool does

        
It builds a consistent black-oil PVT model from routine field inputs — stock-tank oil gravity, separator gas gravity, reservoir temperature and either the solution GOR or the bubble-point pressure — and writes it out in the formats reservoir simulators read.

        
Calculation sequence

        
    
          
  1. Pb from Rsb (or Rsb back-solved from a measured Pb).
    2. 
          
  2. Rs(p) below Pb by numerical inversion of the same Pb equation, so Rs(Pb) = Rsb exactly.
    3. 
          
  3. Bo and μo on the saturated branch from Rs(p).
    4. 
          
  4. Above Pb: Bo = Bob·exp(−∫codp), integrated with the trapezoid rule, and μo from the undersaturated correlation.
    5. 
          
  5. Gas: pseudo-criticals → non-hydrocarbon correction → z → Bg, ρg, cg, μg.
    6. 
          
  6. Water: McCain (1991) Bw and μw, Osif (1988) cw. Salinity is entered in ppm (mg of NaCl per kg of brine) and converted to the weight percent the correlations use: wt % = ppm / 10 000.
    7. 
          
  7. Each PVTO record carries its own saturation pressure and undersaturated branch, which is what ECLIPSE expects for live oil.
    8. 
        

        
The two cursor-linked pictures

        
The barrel and the phase envelope on the Summary tab share one pressure cursor with every chart on the page. Hover a curve, drag the slider, drag the phase diagram, or press Play depletion.

        
    
          
  • Barrel — one stock-tank barrel of oil plus its solution gas at the cursor pressure. Liquid volume is Bo(p); free gas volume is [Rsb − Rs(p)]·Bg(p)/5.614583. Specks in the liquid are dissolved gas (density tracks Rs/Rsb); the cap and the rising bubbles are gas that has come out of solution.
    • 
          
  • Phase envelope — schematic. A true envelope needs a compositional EOS; this one is built to pass exactly through the calculated (Tres, Pb), with the critical temperature placed by a volatility index from Rsb and API gravity. The bubble point is calculated; the shape around it is constructed. Use it to read the fluid type and the depletion path, not to quote a cricondentherm.
    • 
        


        
Gas reservoirs (dry gas and wet gas)

        
Switch Reservoir fluid system to Gas reservoir. Every gas property is reported per standard volume of separator gas — the basis ECLIPSE PVDG and CMG *PVTG use. Both gas types are single-phase in the reservoir at every pressure.

        
    
          
  1. Reservoir-gas gravity from the separator gas recombined with its condensate (McCain, 1990): γw = (Rγg + 4584γo) / (R + 132800γo/Mo), with R = 106/CGR and Mo = 5954/(API − 8.811). A dry gas is its own reservoir gas.
    2. 
          
  2. Pseudo-criticals of γw (Sutton, or Standing’s dry/wet curve) → non-hydrocarbon correction → z, ρg, cg, μg.
    3. 
          
  3. Bg on the separator basis = Bg(well stream) × (1 + Veq·CGR/106), Veq = 132800γo/Mo scf/STB; Eg = 1/Bg.
    4. 
          
  4. Surface condensate from a wet gas is the gas rate × CGR; Rv stays at the CGR at every reservoir pressure.
    5. 
          
  5. Exports: ECLIPSE PVDG, or CMG *MODEL *GASWATER with *PVTG (dry) and *GASWATER_WITH_CONDENSATE with *PVTG *RV (wet).
    6. 
        

        
In gas mode the barrel holds the reservoir gas from 1 Mscf of separator gas, with molecules thinning as it expands. The phase envelope has its critical point at the gas pseudo-critical properties and its cricondentherm below the reservoir temperature, and the separator point shows whether liquid forms at the surface.


        
Laboratory data: none, one report or several

        
The Laboratory data choice in the case inputs sets the workflow. None uses the published correlations. One PVT report checks the study (sampling conditions, trends, Bo–Rs consistency, points out of line), ranks the correlations against it and tunes them to it. Several PVT reports (oil reservoirs) runs the same checks on every sample, compares the samples with each other — each one's bias against the correlation by a robust median/MAD z-score, oil gravity, and for bottomhole samples temperature, Pb and Rsb against the depth trend of the others (Theil–Sen line, leave one out) — and fits one set of multipliers to all the accepted samples together, each at its own Rsb and temperature.

        
Tuning to laboratory data

        
On the Lab data & tuning tab, measured values regress one multiplier per property, in the order the properties depend on each other. Each fit minimises the AARE of its own property over its own points; the published correlation (multiplier 1) is always a candidate, so tuning never worsens the fit.

        
    
          
  1. Rs(p): the curve is stretched in pressure, Pb = Pb,corr × m. A measured Pb sets m exactly; otherwise m is fitted to the Rs points.
    2. 
          
  2. Saturated Bo: Bo = 1 + m(Bo,corr − 1).
    3. 
          
  3. Undersaturated Bo: co is scaled by m.
    4. 
          
  4. Viscosity: a measured dead-oil viscosity scales μod exactly and the saturated points then scale μob; without it, the saturated points scale μod. Above Pb, m scales the rise μo − μob.
    5. 
          
  5. Gas z: Tpc and Ppc are scaled together (coordinate descent), which carries through to Bg, ρg and cg. Gas viscosity is then scaled by m.
    6. 
        

        
The multipliers and the fit before and after tuning are written into the header of every exported deck.


        
Limitations worth stating in a report

        
    
          
  • Correlations carry 5–20% uncertainty on Bo and Rs, and more on viscosity. Check the Correlation spread tab before trusting a single number.
    • 
          
  • In oil mode the gas table is a dry-gas PVDG with no vaporised oil (Rv). Retrograde gas condensates are not modelled in either mode: below a dew point the liquid drop-out and Rv(p) need a CCE/CVD study or a compositional model.
    • 
          
  • The gas-mode CMG deck follows the *GASWATER / *GASWATER_WITH_CONDENSATE keyword set; check it on import into Builder, as CMG keyword sets vary between IMEX versions.
    • 
          
  • Each correlation carries the published applicability range of the data it was fitted to; inputs outside it raise a QC warning on the Summary tab.
    • 
          
  • Tuning adjusts one multiplier per property; it bends a correlation towards the data but cannot change the shape of its curve. A multiplier that stops at its search limit means the correlation does not suit the fluid — rank the correlations and pick another.
    • 
          
  • The phase envelope is a schematic construction anchored to the calculated bubble point — never quote its critical pressure or cricondentherm as data.

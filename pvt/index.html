What this tool does

        
It builds a consistent black-oil PVT model from routine field inputs — stock-tank oil gravity, separator gas gravity, reservoir temperature and either the solution GOR or the bubble-point pressure — and writes it out in the formats reservoir simulators read.

        
Calculation sequence

        
    
          
  1. Pb from Rsb (or Rsb back-solved from a measured Pb).
    2. 
          
  2. Rs(p) below Pb by numerical inversion of the same Pb equation, so Rs(Pb) = Rsb exactly.
    3. 
          
  3. Bo and μo on the saturated branch from Rs(p).
    4. 
          
  4. Above Pb: Bo = Bob·exp(−∫codp), integrated with the trapezoid rule, and μo from the undersaturated correlation.
    5. 
          
  5. Gas: pseudo-criticals → non-hydrocarbon correction → z → Bg, ρg, cg, μg.
    6. 
          
  6. Water: McCain (1991) Bw and μw, Osif (1988) cw.
    7. 
          
  7. Each PVTO record carries its own saturation pressure and undersaturated branch, which is what ECLIPSE expects for live oil.

Limitations worth stating in a report

        
    
          
  • Correlations carry 5–20% uncertainty on Bo and Rs, and more on viscosity. Check the Correlation spread tab before trusting a single number.
    • 
          
  • The gas table is a dry-gas PVDG: no vaporised oil (Rv). For a gas condensate or a volatile oil, use a compositional or modified black-oil model.
    • 
          
  • Each correlation carries the published applicability range of the data it was fitted to; inputs outside it raise a QC warning on the Summary tab.
    • 
          
  • Calibration stretches the correlation to honour measured Pb and Bob; it does not replace a laboratory CCE/DL study.
